# Rank-six boundary branch: scheme-level smooth-center proof

The rank-five theorem is part of the [earlier normalization theorem](../../proofs/STATEMENTS.md); it is not a new result. The result considered here is the exact total and regularity of the previously unresolved rank-six normalization fiber.

The mathematical dependencies are the [baseline proof](../../../proofs/PROOF.md), the earlier [normalization statements](../../proofs/STATEMENTS.md) and [normalization proof](../../proofs/NORMALIZATION_FIBERS.md), and the [family derivation](../../../families/proofs/REACH_DERIVATION.md).

## 1. Exact target

Take the existing r=6 REACH tensor with minimal first/second supports (p,q)=(4,3), or its transpose (3,4). Write

    L = L0 direct-sum C R,   R=u tensor v(4),

where u is the new A-direction and L0 is the five-dimensional span of the six circuit matrices Qi. Pad into dimensions a>=4,b>=3,c>=6 with a+b>=8 (or transpose these conditions). The existing result proves that P(L) meets the matrix Segre in exactly the seven reduced points Q1,...,Q6,R; there are exactly six actual decompositions, and at least seven normalization points. It leaves exact total and boundary smoothness open.

Claim proved below: there are exactly SEVEN normalization points, all smooth, and all seven fibers are reduced/unramified. The seventh point corresponds to the six dependent core directions and is not an ordinary six-term decomposition. There is an open neighborhood of the tensor whose entire normalization is smooth and unramified.

## 2. Dependence center in the six-point configuration space

Let S=Seg(PA x PB), dim A=a, dim B=b, W=A tensor B. Work first in the ordered distinct-six-point configuration space C6, smooth of dimension

    n=6(a+b-2).

Let z=(Q1,...,Q6) be the ordered core configuration. Restrict to an affine neighborhood where the first five points stay independent and where local nonzero rank-one representatives mi are chosen. Select a fixed five-row pivot of the W-by-6 matrix (m1,...,m6) which is invertible on the first five columns. Subtract the unique linear combination of m1,...,m5 that matches m6 on those pivot rows; call the residual vector q. The locus D of rank at most five has ideal I generated by the remaining coordinates of q. This is its determinantal scheme, not merely its underlying set.

Equivalently, introduce a relation lambda=(lambda1,...,lambda5,1), requiring

    sum_i lambda_i mi=0.                                  (2.1)

The selected pivot solves lambda1,...,lambda5 uniquely and regularly. Eliminating them identifies this incidence scheme with D. At z the unique relation has all lambda_i nonzero. Shrink so all remain units on the relation incidence.

Lift local Segre points to nonzero factor vectors ai,bi, and form matrices A=(a1,...,a6), B=(b1,...,b6). This lift is a smooth torus torsor of relative dimension12. Absorbing the units lambda_i into ai transforms (2.1) to

    A B^t=0,                                               (2.2)

with the five lambda coordinates otherwise free. At the core, A and B both have rank three. Their first/last three columns supply the pivots used below.

## 3. Explicit smooth scheme chart for AB^t=0

Split the middle six-dimensional space into its first and last three coordinates:

    A=[A1 A2],       B^t=[B1; B2].

At the core A1 has column rank3 and B2 has row rank3. Select fixed three A-rows so the resulting 3-by-3 submatrix of A1 is invertible. Select fixed three B-columns so the resulting submatrix C of B2 is invertible. Write

    B1=[D E],       B2=[C F],

where C,D are 3-by-3, E,F are 3-by-(b-3). Then the equation is

    [A1 D + A2 C, A1 E + A2 F]=0.

Since C is invertible, the first block solves

    A2=-A1 D C^(-1).

Substituting this in the second block gives

    A1 (E-D C^(-1)F)=0.

The selected invertible minor of A1 supplies a left inverse, so this is scheme-theoretically equivalent to

    E=D C^(-1)F.

Thus the full ideal AB^t=0 on this chart is generated by equations solving A2 and E in terms of the free coordinates A1,C,D,F. There are no remaining equations. The chart is smooth of dimension

    3a+9+9+3(b-3)=3a+3b+9.

Adding the five free lambda coordinates and descending through the 12-dimensional smooth torus torsor proves that D is smooth of dimension3a+3b+2. Its codimension in C6 is

    h = 3a+3b-14.                                          (3.1)

The exact eliminated residual ideal I is therefore the reduced smooth-center ideal. This avoids the gap of proving only that its underlying zero set is smooth.

## 4. Differential image and exceptional directions

The differential of the residual q at z is, up to a harmless common sign,

    (delta m1,...,delta m6) |-> sum_i delta mi modulo L0.

The tangent spaces to the rank-one cone at Qi=ai tensor bi sum to

    U = A0 tensor B + A tensor B0,

because the ai span A0 and the bi span B0. This space has dimension

    dim U = 3a+3b-9.

Radial rescaling directions lie in L0 and disappear in the quotient, so the derivative image from projective point variations is exactly U/L0, of dimension h. Its kernel is T_z D, by Section3. Consequently the induced normal-space map is an isomorphism

    N_(D/C6),z  -> U/L0.                                   (4.1)

## 5. Graph of span is the blowup of D

The rational span map near z is given in Plucker coordinates by

    m1 wedge ... wedge m5 wedge m6
      = m1 wedge ... wedge m5 wedge q.

The chosen pivot makes its base ideal exactly I, the ideal generated by the coordinates of q. The closure of the graph of a rational map given by generators of an ideal is Proj of its Rees algebra, i.e. the blowup Bl_I(C6). This follows directly on affine charts: the Rees algebra embeds into R[t], so its Proj is the reduced closure of the ordinary graph where I is invertible. The image already lies in the Grassmannian by the Plucker relations, so taking the graph in the Grassmannian gives the same model.

Because C6 and D are smooth, this blowup is smooth. At z its exceptional fiber is P(N_(D/C6),z)=P^(h-1). Under the map to Gr(6,W), (4.1) sends the exceptional point [v] to

    L0 + C v.

This is injective on the exceptional fiber: distinct lines of W/L0 give distinct six-dimensional subspaces containing L0.

Now R=u tensor v(4) lies in A tensor B0 subset U, and R is not in L0. Therefore exactly ONE exceptional point lies over the pair (z,L0+C R). It is smooth in the graph closure. The free permutation quotient does not change local smoothness at the corresponding unordered distinct configuration: the ordering cover is etale.

## 6. Complete proper fiber and normalization

Use the proper Hilbert graph model: K is the reduced closure of the span graph of independent reduced length-six subschemes of S in Hilb^6(S) x Gr(6,W). The incidence condition Z subset P(K') is closed and holds throughout K. Over the span L, every length-six fiber scheme is therefore contained in

    S intersect P(L) = {Q1,...,Q6,R},

a reduced seven-point scheme. Such a scheme is a six-element subset. There are only seven candidates. The six which omit a core Q-point are independent, hence are present and smooth ordinary graph points. The seventh, which omits R, is precisely the unique smooth exceptional point of Section5. Thus the complete proper fiber consists of seven smooth points.

For scheme structures, the fiber is a closed subscheme of Hilb^6(S intersect P(L)); the latter is the reduced discrete scheme of seven six-element subsets. Since all seven occur, the fiber itself is reduced. This also proves that the graph-to-span differential has zero kernel at all seven points. Alternatively, tangent containment forces all point variations to vanish because S intersect P(L) is reduced, and (4.1) gives injectivity in the exceptional direction.

Let H be the reduced image of the span morphism in Gr(6,W). The admissible dimension assumptions imply its generic unordered uniqueness, as proved in the existing REACH result, so K->H is proper birational. A finite fiber becomes finite after target shrinking; the nonsmooth locus has proper closed image avoiding L and can be removed. On the resulting neighborhood K->H is finite birational with smooth source, hence is the complete normalization. Reduced fibers at L show unramifiedness there; removing the proper image of the ramification locus gives a neighborhood where the normalization is smooth and unramified.

Finally, the rank-six third-flattening locus of X_6(a,b,c) is the surjection bundle C* -> K' over H. Pulling back the finite smooth normalization gives a finite birational normal model of this tensor locus, hence its complete normalization. The fiber above the specified T has exactly seven reduced points, all smooth of dimension6(a+b+c-2). The six actual decompositions and the one boundary point are exactly as claimed.

## 7. Scope and limitation

This verifies r=6 with one added row or one added column, in every stated admissible padding. It does not by itself prove r>=7, where the dependent configuration retains other added points and the dependence center has zero relation coefficients at those points. Absorbing all relation coefficients as units is then unavailable; a separate local calculation is required. It also does not prove global smoothness or global Cohen--Macaulayness of the normalization.
