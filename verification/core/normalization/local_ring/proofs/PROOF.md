# Exact gluing of the six rank-five tensor branches

All fields are C. Varieties and image closures carry reduced structure.

## 1. Exact statement

Let a,b≥3, a+b≥7, and c≥5. Write X=X_5(a,b,c) for the affine variety of
border-rank-at-most-five tensors in A⊗B⊗C, with dimensions a,b,c. Fix
three-dimensional coordinate subspaces A0⊂A and B0⊂B, and set

    M = [[1,1,1],[1,2,3],[1,4,9]],
    Q_i = e_i ⊗ row_i(M)                 (1≤i≤3),
    Q_(3+j) = −col_j(M) ⊗ e_j            (1≤j≤3),
    T = Σ_(i=1)^5 Q_i ⊗ f_i.

Here f_1,...,f_5 are coordinate vectors of C. Put

    q = 2(a+b−6),             h = 3a+3b+5c+2.

Then the exact local description is

    completed O_(X,T) ≅
      C[[z_1,...,z_h, x_(i,j) : 1≤i≤6, 1≤j≤q]] /
        (x_(i,j)x_(k,l) : i≠k).                              (1)

Consequently

    dim O_(X,T)   = h+q = 5(a+b+c−2),
    depth O_(X,T) = h+1 = 3a+3b+5c+3,
    embedding dimension = h+6q,
    Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity = 6.                         (2)

In particular, the original padded 6×6×6 witness has dimension80 and
depth69. In format3×4×5 it has dimension50 and depth49. The ring in (1)
has six smooth formal branches; every pair meets the same smooth reduced
h-dimensional formal germ. Its conductor into its normalization is the
ideal generated by all x_(i,j). This is a local statement at the specified
tensor. It gives no vertex-depth formula or global normalization theorem.

## 2. The core linear section

Let L0=span(Q_1,...,Q_6)⊂W0=A0⊗B0. Its dimension is five, the sole
linear relation is ΣQ_i=0, and every five Q_i are independent. Indeed,
an arbitrary relation has entries M_ij(u_i−v_j)=0; all M_ij are nonzero,
so all six coefficients are equal.

We record the finite linear-section fact used below, together with its
elementary verification. The scheme P(L0)∩Seg(PA0×PB0) consists of exactly
the six reduced points [Q_i]. A matrix in L0 has entries
N_ij=M_ij(u_i−v_j). If a rank-one N=ab^t has a zero factor coordinate,
one zero row or column makes N one of the displayed row or column
directions: the other rows or columns of M are independent. If all factor
coordinates are nonzero, normalize a=(1,u,v), b=(1,s,t). Membership gives

    E1=(u−2)s−2(u−1),       E2=(u−3)t−3(u−1),
    E3=(v−4)s−4(v−1),       E4=(v−9)t−9(v−1),

all zero. But the polynomial identity

    3(u−4)((u−2)E3−(v−4)E1)
      −(u−3)((u−3)E4−(v−9)E2) = 6u(u−1)

forces u=1 and then s=0, a contradiction. At the three row and three
column points, scalar-only affine tangent intersections follow from the
respective nonzero ratio determinants

    (−1/18, 1/3, −2),       (−1/18, 4/9, −3/2).

The ratio matrices have first row (1,1,1) and other rows equal to the
chosen row divided entrywise by each other row of M; columns use the
transposed construction. Substitution into N_ij=M_ij(u_i−v_j) shows that
the other two rows, or columns, vanish. Thus each projective intersection
has zero tangent space. Its local maximal ideal satisfies m/m²=0, so
Nakayama proves reducedness. These identities are replayed from the
exact checker; the geometric argument is a written proof.

## 3. A six-section etale core chart

Let G0=Gr(5,W0), a smooth variety of dimension20, and let

    Z0={(L,p)∈G0×Seg(PA0×PB0): p∈P(L)}.

Projection of Z0 to the Segre is a Grassmann bundle: its fiber over a
line p is Gr(4,W0/p), of dimension16. Hence Z0 is smooth of dimension20.
Its projection to G0 is proper. The fiber at L0 is the six reduced points
from Section2. After shrinking G0, properness and upper semicontinuity
make this projection finite. It is unramified at each of those points,
since the relative cotangent space is the cotangent space of the reduced
point fiber. As source and target are smooth of the same dimension,
the differential is an isomorphism there, so the map is etale there.
Removing the proper image of its non-etale locus makes it finite etale
near the whole fiber, of degree six.

After an etale base change near L0, the six sections can therefore be
labeled p_1,...,p_6. All subsequent arguments take place on a sufficiently
small smooth etale neighborhood U0 with chosen complex point over L0.
Completing at that point gives the same complete local ring as G0 at L0.
Trivialize the six line bundles, and choose regular nonzero representatives

    q_i=a_i⊗b_i ∈ L,             Σ_i q_i=0.                    (3)

To arrange (3), first choose any representatives. Every five are a basis
near L0, so their unique relation has all coefficients invertible; absorb
these coefficients into the representatives and one factor. Factorization
into a_i,b_i is regular after choosing a nonzero entry of each rank-one
matrix. The lists a_i and b_i continue to span A0 and B0.

No flatness of the larger-format point-incidence is assumed here. The
etaleness just proved concerns the smooth twenty-dimensional core chart.

## 4. Nearby five-planes as graphs

Choose complements A=A0⊕U and B=B0⊕V, of dimensions alpha=a−3 and
beta=b−3. Projection to W0 identifies every five-plane sufficiently
near L0 with the graph over its projected L∈G0 of three linear maps

    F:L→U⊗B0,    G:L→A0⊗V,    K:L→U⊗V.                    (4)

This gives an open Grassmannian chart, pulled back to U0. A rank-one point
of the graph projects to a nonzero rank-one point of L, so its projective
image must be one of the p_i. The graph is injective under projection;
there is at most one lifted line over each p_i. Conversely, the lift of
q_i is rank one exactly when there exist u_i∈U and v_i∈V such that

    F(q_i)=u_i⊗b_i,     G(q_i)=a_i⊗v_i,
    K(q_i)=u_i⊗v_i.                                         (5)

This follows simply by expanding (a_i+u_i)⊗(b_i+v_i). A rank-one lift
whose core part is exactly q_i admits precisely this factorization;
reciprocal rescaling of its factors does not alter (5).

## 5. Residue coordinates and the common locus

Consider the vector-bundle map

    Phi_A: Hom(L,U⊗B0) → ⊕_(i=1)^6 U⊗(B0/<b_i>),
                  F ↦ (F(q_i) mod U⊗b_i)_i.                (6)

It is surjective. Indeed, specify arbitrary quotient classes and choose
representatives w_i∈U⊗B0. Because the b_i span B0, choose u_i∈U such
that Σ_i u_i⊗b_i=−Σ_i w_i. The six adjusted vectors have sum zero.
By (3) and the independence of every five q_i, they are the values of a
unique linear map F on L. This proves surjectivity also when alpha=0.

The kernel has rank15alpha−12alpha=3alpha. It consists precisely of

    F=(P⊗id_B0)|L,             P∈Hom(A0,U).                 (7)

These maps lie in the kernel, and the map P↦F is injective because the
a_i span A0. The dimensions therefore establish equality. In the same
way, the analogous map Phi_B for G is surjective to
⊕_(i=1)^6 (A0/<a_i>)⊗V, with kernel

    G=(id_A0⊗Q)|L,             Q∈Hom(B0,V).                 (8)

After shrinking U0, split these surjections of vector bundles and choose
frames of their rank-two quotient factors. Equations (6)–(8) give
coordinates P,Q and six residue groups x_i, each of dimension

    2alpha+2beta=q.

The first two equations in (5) hold for every index other than i if and
only if x_k=0 for k≠i. When every residue vanishes they hold for all six,
with u_i=P(a_i), v_i=Q(b_i). Then all six last equations of (5) are
consistent because applying P⊗Q to (3) gives Σ_i u_i⊗v_i=0.
The common value of K is

    H0=(P⊗Q)|L.                                             (9)

The common locus is therefore smooth of dimension20+3alpha+3beta.
This is an exact family calculation, not only a tangent-space count.

## 6. Each configuration branch and simultaneous straightening

Fix i. On the residue axis x_k=0 for k≠i, require the five points with
indices k≠i to lift. Their q_k form a basis of L. The first two equations
of (5) determine u_k,v_k uniquely, and the last equations determine K
uniquely on that basis. Write this regular map as

    K=H_i(L,P,Q,x_i).

Regularity follows by inverting the basis determinant and the selected
nonzero factor entries, all units on our neighborhood. It is bilinear
in the F/G variables over the core chart. At x_i=0, every H_i agrees
with H0 by (9). Thus the locus B_i containing these five lifted lines
is an embedded smooth closed graph, with coordinates L,P,Q,x_i.

Extend H_i to all residue coordinates by ignoring x_k for k≠i, and put

    H=H0+Σ_(i=1)^6 (H_i−H0),       K'=K−H.                  (10)

Translation (10) is an invertible change of the ambient graph coordinates.
On the i-th residue axis H=H_i. Consequently

    B_i = {K'=0, x_k=0 for every k≠i}.                      (11)

This proves collective independence of the normal directions and the
scheme-theoretic pair-intersection assertion simultaneously. It does not
infer such assertions merely from pairwise transversality.

## 7. These six branches exhaust the local span variety

Let S=Seg(PA×PB), and H⊂Gr(5,A⊗B) be the reduced closure of spans of
five independent points of S. We justify that the reduced local germ
of H is exactly the union of the six B_i, including possible limits.

The point-incidence Z={(L,p):p∈P(L)∩S} is proper over the Grassmannian.
Its fiber at the padded L0 is again exactly the six reduced points:
padding creates neither new rank-one directions nor extra tangent
directions in the fiber. Thus after shrinking it is finite and unramified
over an ambient neighborhood of L0. The argument uses the relative
cotangent criterion, then removes the proper image of the complement of
the unramified locus; it does not assert flatness.

In the fivefold fiber product of Z, the locus D of pairwise distinct
points is both open and closed: the diagonal of a separated unramified
map is open and closed. Hence D is finite over the ambient neighborhood.
Every tuple over L0 is independent; removing the closed image of the
nonindependent locus makes every tuple in D independent. The image of D
is closed and is exactly the locus of five-planes spanned by five distinct
Segre points on this neighborhood. It therefore equals H there: it is
contained in H, and contains the dense defining span locus of H restricted
to the neighborhood. Closure commutes with restriction to this open set.

On our graph chart all rank-one points are the possible lifts of the six
labeled core points. Every choice of five has independent core projections.
Thus the image of D is exactly ⋃_(i=1)^6 B_i. The etale pullback of H is
reduced, and each B_i is smooth and reduced; equality of their closed
sets is therefore equality of these reduced schemes. This excludes extra
branches from coincident or dependent limiting configurations.

By (11), eliminating K' identifies the local span germ with the union of
the six coordinate q-planes over the smooth base (L,P,Q), whose dimension
is20+3alpha+3beta. Its ideal is generated by every product of coordinates
from two different residue groups. Indeed a monomial lies in the
intersection of the six coordinate branch ideals precisely when its
support meets at least two groups. This argument applies to the completed
power-series rings as well, or follows by flatness of completion from the
finite intersection of these ideals.

## 8. Passing from five-planes to tensors

The flattening C*→A⊗B of T has rank five. On the open rank-five locus,
X is the bundle of surjections C*↠L over H. To see this, over each span
of five independent rank-one matrices every such surjection has a
five-term tensor decomposition. Conversely, a rank-five five-term tensor
decomposition must have independent first-two-mode matrices and independent
third factors. These tensors form a dense subset, and taking closure
commutes with this smooth, open bundle projection. Hence the reduced
rank-five locus is exactly the indicated bundle over H.

The bundle fiber is an open subset of Hom(C*,L), of dimension5c. Near T
it is smooth and locally trivial. It adds5c formal parameters to the
span germ of Section7. Since U0 is smooth of dimension20 and its etale
base change preserves the completed local ring at the chosen complex
point, this proves (1), with

    h=20+3alpha+3beta+5c=3a+3b+5c+2.

## 9. Depth, multiplicity and conductor

Let R_q=C[[x_(i,j)]]/(cross-group products). It embeds in
B=∏_(i=1)^6 C[[x_(i,1),...,x_(i,q)]] as exactly the tuples with a common
constant term: every series in R_q is uniquely a constant plus six series
supported on a single group and having zero constant term. Therefore

    0 → R_q → B → C^5 → 0,                                 (12)

where the last map takes the first five constant terms minus the sixth.
The ring is reduced of dimensionq. As an R_q-module B has depthq:
each direct summand is a regular q-dimensional quotient, and the depth
of a finite direct sum is the minimum. The module C^5 has depth0.
Since q≥2, the depth lemma applied to (12) gives depth R_q=1.

The common z variables form a regular sequence in R_q[[z_1,...,z_h]].
Successive quotients remove one variable; multiplication by a formal
variable is injective over any coefficient ring. Thus (1) has depthh+1
and dimensionh+q. Dimension and depth are preserved by completion of
the Noetherian local ring O_(X,T), proving the first two formulas in (2).

All defining equations in (1) are quadratic, so its embedding dimension
is h+6q. Its associated graded Hilbert series is

    (1−t)^(-h) (6(1−t)^(-q)−5).

Multiplying by (1−t)^(h+q) and setting t=1 gives multiplicity6.

The normalization of the completed ring is the product of the six
regular branch rings C[[z,x_i]]. Their tuples in (1) have a common
restriction to C[[z]]. Multiplication by a branch idempotent shows that
an element is in the conductor if and only if all those restrictions
are zero. Hence the conductor is the radical ideal (all x_(i,j)), with
quotient C[[z]]. Every two branches meet this same reduced smooth germ.

In particular the failure of Cohen–Macaulayness is quantified by
dim−depth=q−1. This does not mean depth at T is below two: its value is
h+1. The local codimension-q common branch locus is where the S2 defect
is supported in this model.

## 10. Dependencies and novelty boundary

The circuit, its finite reduced Segre intersection, and the existence of
six smooth normalization points are established in the
[normalization-fibre proof](../../proofs/NORMALIZATION_FIBERS.md). Section2 reproduces the core argument, and
Section7 re-establishes branch exhaustion without relying on an unproved
global normalization description. The new step is the simultaneous
scheme-theoretic graph description (6)–(11), followed by the exact local
depth calculation. Standard inputs are Grassmannian charts, the proper
quasi-finite theorem, the unramified and etale differential criteria,
etale preservation of reducedness/completions, and the depth lemma.

This is a natural next question from the explicit unresolved-depth scope
of the earlier TR-25 result, not a claim to solve another named published
conjecture.
