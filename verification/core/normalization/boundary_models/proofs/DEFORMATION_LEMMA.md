# Normality under a regular-parameter deformation

All rings in this note contain C. Normalization is taken in the fraction field of an integral domain.

## Lemma

Let D be a local domain obtained by localizing a finitely generated C-domain, and let 0!=f belong to its maximal ideal. If D/fD is a normal domain, then D is normal.

## Proof

Let N be the normalization of D. It is finite over D: finite-type C-domains have finite normalization, and normalization commutes with localization. The ideal (f) is prime by hypothesis. It has height one by the principal ideal theorem, since D is a domain and f is nonzero and not a unit. Thus D_(f) is a one-dimensional local domain with principal maximal ideal generated by f. It is a regular local ring, hence a discrete valuation ring and integrally closed.

Every element of N is integral over D_(f), so N embeds in D_(f). Localizing N at D minus (f) gives the normalization of D_(f), which is D_(f) itself. Consequently there is exactly one prime Q of N contracting to (f), and N_Q=D_(f). In particular f has valuation one at Q.

Every height-one prime of N containing f contracts to (f). Indeed, finite birational extensions of affine C-domains preserve prime heights: their total dimensions agree, and corresponding residue fields have equal transcendence degree because the residue extension is finite. The dimension formula therefore gives equality of the heights. This persists after the localizations defining D and N. A height-one contraction containing the height-one prime (f) must equal (f). Hence Q is the only height-one prime of N containing f.

Reduction in N_Q=D_(f) embeds N/Q into Frac(D/fD). The ring N/Q is integral over D/fD. Since D/fD is normal in that fraction field,

    N/Q=D/fD.

Given n in N, choose d in D with the same reduction modulo Q. The fraction c=(n-d)/f has nonnegative valuation at Q: its numerator has valuation at least one and its denominator has valuation one. At every other height-one prime of N, f is a unit and n-d lies in N, so c again has nonnegative valuation. A Noetherian normal domain is the intersection of its height-one valuation rings in its fraction field. Therefore c belongs to N.

It follows that N=D+fN. The finite D-module N/D satisfies N/D=f(N/D). Nakayama's lemma, applied because f is in the maximal ideal, gives N/D=0. Thus D=N is normal. ∎

## Graded consequence

Let E be a finitely generated nonnegatively graded C-domain, and let xi be a nonzero homogeneous element of positive degree. Suppose E/(xi) is a normal domain. Then E is normal.

At every prime containing xi, the lemma applied to the local ring proves normality. The nonnormal locus of a finite-type C-ring is closed. It is invariant under the multiplicative action associated with the grading, since that action consists of automorphisms for nonzero scalars. Choose a finite homogeneous set of algebra generators, all of nonnegative degree. Every closed point has an affine limit under scalar multiplication as the scalar tends to zero: its positive-degree generator coordinates become zero, and its degree-zero coordinates remain fixed. This limit lies on xi=0.

If the nonnormal locus were nonempty, it would contain a closed point, its entire nonzero-scalar orbit, and its limit on xi=0. This contradicts normality there. Therefore E is normal everywhere. The nonnegative grading is essential to this argument; local normality along one fiber of an arbitrary nonproper family would not by itself give the same conclusion. ∎
