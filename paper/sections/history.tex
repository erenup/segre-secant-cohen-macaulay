\subsection{The conjecture and established cases}\label{sec:history}

Landsberg and Weyman asked whether secant varieties of Segre varieties
are arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay
\cite[Introduction, p.~3 of the arXiv version]{LandsbergWeyman2007}.
Oeding formulated the universal assertion as Conjecture~1.1
of~\cite{Oeding2016}.
Jagie\l\l a and Jelisiejew restated it as Conjecture~3.32
of~\cite{JagiellaJelisiejew2026}, explicitly identifying arithmetic
Cohen--Macaulayness with Cohen--Macaulayness of the affine cone.
Both formulations concern the ordinary reduced secant variety of
a Segre product of projective spaces. They impose neither a
conciseness condition nor a restriction to balanced formats.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} refutes this assertion with the same field,
reduced structure, and projective embedding.

Several substantial positive families are known. Landsberg and Weyman
proved arithmetic Cohen--Macaulayness for all secants of
$\PP^1\times\PP^{b-1}\times\PP^{c-1}$ with $b,c\geq2$, second secants
of four Segre factors of vector dimensions at least two, and third
secants of three Segre factors of vector dimensions at least three
\cite[Theorems~1.1--1.3]{LandsbergWeyman2007}.
For the first family their proof gives the affine cone rational
singularities, and hence the arithmetic conclusion.
Catalisano, Geramita and Gimigliano proved further unbalanced cases
by identifying the secants with generic determinantal varieties
\cite[Theorem~2.4]{CatalisanoGeramitaGimigliano2008}. For Segre
factors of vector dimensions $d_1,\ldots,d_t,d$ and
$m=d_1\cdots d_t$, they proved that if
\[
 m-(d_1-1)-\cdots-(d_t-1)\leq r\leq\min\{d-1,\,m-1\},
\]
then $\sigma_r(\Seg(\PP^{d_1-1}\times\cdots\times\PP^{d_t-1}\times\PP^{d-1}))$
coincides with $\sigma_r(\Seg(\PP^{m-1}\times\PP^{d-1}))$, its ideal is
generated by the $(r+1)\times(r+1)$ minors of a $d\times m$ matrix of
indeterminates, and it is arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay. For three
factors of vector dimensions $a,b,c$ this is the range
$(a-1)(b-1)+1\leq r\leq\min\{c-1,ab-1\}$.
Oeding proved that $\sigma_4(\operatorname{Seg}(\PP^2\times\PP^2
\times\PP^n))$ is arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay for every $n\geq3$
\cite[Theorem~3.2]{Oeding2016}.
For arbitrary numbers of Segre factors, Raicu proved that the ideal
of the secant-line variety is generated by the cubic flattening
minors \cite[Corollary~4.2]{Raicu2012}; this is an ideal-generation
result, not an arithmetic Cohen--Macaulayness assertion.
These results describe selected positive cases and known equations,
not a classification of the Cohen--Macaulay secants.

\subsection{Comparison with nonnormality}\label{sec:nonnormality}

For $r\geq1$, let
$\Delta_r=\sum_{i=1}^r e_i\otimes e_i\otimes e_i$.
B\"urgisser and Ikenmeyer proved that the unit-tensor orbit closure
\[
 \overline{\bigl(\GL_r(\C)\bigr)^3\cdot\Delta_r}
 \subseteq (\C^r)^{\otimes3}
\]
is not normal for every $r\geq5$
\cite[Corollary~7.2(2)]{BurgisserIkenmeyer2011}; their later treatment
repeats the result \cite[Corollary~5.26]{BurgisserIkenmeyer2017}.
The corollary numbers refer to the arXiv versions specified in the
bibliography. The orbit closure equals $X_r(r,r,r)$: the invertible
triples of factor matrices form a dense open subset of the
parametrizing space for sums of $r$ pure tensors.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore strengthens the known nonnormality
of these same orbit closures to failure of $S_2$ and of
Cohen--Macaulayness, for every $r\geq5$.

Nonnormality alone does not imply either conclusion. For example,
$\C[t^2,t^3]\simeq\C[u,v]/(v^2-u^3)$ is a Cohen--Macaulay
hypersurface domain that is not normal: $t$ is integral over the
ring but does not belong to it. Serre's normality criterion separates
the conditions $R_1$ and $S_2$ \cite[Tag~031S]{stacks-project}.
The proof below detects failure of $S_2$ through the conductor of
the finite normalization; the cited nonnormality results are not
assumptions in that proof.
